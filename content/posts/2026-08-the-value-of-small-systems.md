Good decisions are rarely the result of one impressive moment. More often, they come from small systems that make the useful action easier to repeat.

## Reduce the number of dramatic decisions

A simple checklist, a regular review, or a clear rule can remove unnecessary effort from everyday choices. This applies to financial habits, technical work, and the way we protect time for thinking.

The purpose of a system is not to remove judgment. It is to reserve judgment for the decisions that actually deserve it.

## Make the useful action visible

A system works best when it is easy to see and easy to begin. A short note, a small queue of ideas, or a weekly review can be enough to turn an intention into a practice.

The result is modest but valuable: less noise, fewer avoidable decisions, and more attention available for the work that matters.
